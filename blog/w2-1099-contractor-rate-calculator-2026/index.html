Why "Salary ÷ 2080 × 1.5" Is Dangerously Wrong

The "multiply by 1.5" rule assumes two things: your benefits cost 33% of salary, and you're billing 100% of your time. Both are false. Real contractor overhead includes self-employment tax (full 15.3%), unsubsidized health insurance ($8-18k), lost 401k match (3-6%), zero paid leave, equity forfeiture, business overhead, and income volatility. The actual multiplier ranges from 1.4x (junior, no-tax state, lean benefits) to 2.2x (senior, CA/NY, full benefits).

When Contracting Actually Pays More

The math above is your floor — the rate to match your W-2 total compensation. Contracting wins when:

  • High-demand skills: DevOps, AI/ML, Security, Data Engineering — 2-3x multiplier achievable because clients pay for outcomes, not hours.
  • Short-term specialized engagements: No bench time, no ramp-up, defined scope. Fractional CTO/VP Eng roles at $250-500/hr.
  • Equity-free compensation preference: You want cash now, not lottery tickets. Contracting converts equity risk into rate premium.
  • Geographic arbitrage: Live in Texas (0% tax, low COL), bill SF/NYC rates. A Senior Dev in Austin billing $180/hr to NYC clients clears $300k+ after overhead.
  • Multiple concurrent clients: Diversifies revenue risk. 2-3 retainers at 20 hrs/week each > 1 full-time W-2.

The Contractor Checklist (Before You Sign)

  1. Run your numbers in the calculator. Don't negotiate from gut feel. Open calculator →
  2. Get health insurance quotes. ACA marketplace, COBRA (18 months), spouse's plan, or healthshare. Budget $600-1,500/mo individual; $1,500-3,000/mo family.
  3. Confirm retirement strategy. Solo 401k ($69k limit 2026), SEP-IRA (25% of net), or Roth IRA. Max them before taxable brokerage.
  4. Add 10-20% volatility buffer to your rate. Gap time between contracts is real. 15% default in our calculator.
  5. Negotiate Corp-to-Corp vs 1099. C2C shifts liability, allows expense deductions, but client may resist. 1099 is simpler.
  6. Set up quarterly estimated tax payments. IRS Form 1040-ES. Due April 15, June 15, Sept 15, Jan 15. Penalty for underpayment: ~8% annualized.
  7. Contract review: IP ownership, non-compete, payment terms. Net-30 is standard; push for Net-15 or upfront deposit. Kill non-competes or limit to 6 months / specific competitor list.

FAQ

How do I convert my W-2 salary to a 1099 contractor rate?
Don't just multiply by 1.5. Use: (Target Salary + Benefits + Overhead + Self-Employment Tax) / (Billable Hours). The 7 cost categories are: SE tax (15.3% vs 7.65%), health insurance ($8-18k), 401k match loss (3-6%), paid leave (10-20 days), equity forfeiture, overhead ($3-8k), and income volatility buffer (10-20%). Our calculator handles all line items automatically.
What is the 1099 equivalent of a $100k W-2 salary?
In a no-tax state (TX/FL/WA) with standard benefits: ~$72-78/hr ($150-162k annualized) — a 1.5-1.6x multiplier. In high-tax states (CA/NY) with full benefits: ~$85-95/hr ($177-198k) — a 1.75-1.9x multiplier. The exact number depends on your health plan, 401k match, and billable ratio. Use our calculator for your specific situation.
Do contractors pay more taxes than employees?
Yes. Employees pay 7.65% FICA (Social Security + Medicare); contractors pay 15.3% SE tax on the first $168,600 (2026 limit) and 2.9% Medicare above that. Contractors also pay both sides of FICA but can deduct the employer half (7.65%) as a business expense, reducing the effective hit to ~14.1% on the first $168,600.
What benefits do I lose going from W-2 to 1099?
Employer-subsidized health insurance (saves $6-16k/yr), 401k match (3-6% = $3.6-10.8k on $180k), paid leave (10-20 days = $4.6-9.2k), equity refresh grants, disability/life insurance, HSA/FSA contributions, commuter benefits, and legal/HR protection. You also lose unemployment insurance eligibility in most states.
How much should I charge as a contractor vs employee?
Minimum floor: (Your W-2 total compensation + SE tax delta + health + 401k match + paid leave value + overhead) / (Billable hours). Typical multipliers: Junior (1.4-1.6x), Mid (1.6-1.8x), Senior (1.8-2.2x). High-demand skills (DevOps, AI/ML, Security) can command 2-3x. Always add 10-20% volatility buffer for gap time between contracts.

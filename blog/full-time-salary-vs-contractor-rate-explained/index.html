The Salary Conversion Myth

Many professionals mistakenly calculate their desired hourly rate by dividing their annual salary by 2,080 hours (40 hours/week × 52 weeks). This approach ignores critical freelance realities:

  • No paid vacation or sick days
  • Health insurance and retirement contributions
  • Business expenses (software, internet, equipment)
  • Client acquisition and administrative time
  • Self-employment taxes (15.3% on net income)

The True Cost Breakdown

To calculate your true contractor rate, use this comprehensive formula:

$$\text{Hourly Rate} = \frac{\text{Target Annual Income} + \text{Annual Business Expenses} + \text{Self-Employment Taxes} + \text{Benefits Value}}{\text{Billable Hours Per Year}}$$

Key Differences

Factor Employee Contractor
Health Insurance Employer-covered Self-paid (marketplace or spouse)
Paid Time Off 20+ days/year 0 days (unpaid)
Retirement 401(k) match Self-directed (SEP-IRA, Solo 401k)
Tax Withholding Automatic (employer) Quarterly estimated payments
Work Hours 40 hours/week (fixed) Variable (based on project needs)

Practical Example

If you earn $80,000 as a salaried employee, your true contractor rate might be $120-150/hour when accounting for:

  • 25% for health insurance and retirement
  • 15% for self-employment taxes
  • 10% for business expenses
  • 20% for unpaid time (prospecting, admin)
